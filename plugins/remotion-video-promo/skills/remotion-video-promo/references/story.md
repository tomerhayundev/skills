# Story, script and copy: the core

The narrative, the script and the words on screen decide whether a video works. The motion delivers
them: a beautiful film with a vague message fails, and a sharp script carries simple animation. So
every word is written, read aloud, timed and approved before anything moves.

## 1. The message

Write these three lines before anything else, and keep them at the top of `docs/script.md`:

- **Who**: the viewer, and what they believe or struggle with right now.
- **The one message**: one sentence the viewer should repeat after watching. Not a list.
- **The change**: what they should believe or do afterwards (the call to action follows from it).

If the message needs "and", there are two videos.

## 2. The narrative

A video is a change, not a list of features: something is wrong, something turns, it is better. The
format's module gives the beat shape (promo: hook, reveal, highlights, close; tutorial: outcome, steps,
recap; explainer: problem, why it matters, idea, how it works, proof). Inside that shape:

- **Tension first**: the problem, the cost, the before. The product is the turn, not the opening.
  Where the module's story shape says otherwise, it wins: a tutorial opens on the outcome (its viewer
  arrived from search with the problem already), and its tension is saved for the turn.
- **One turn**: the moment the product changes things; it lands on the music's lift or the voice's
  emphasis, and the motif does it (the needle strikes, the slot splits).
- **Show, then say**: the picture proves what the words claim. A caption never describes what is
  already obvious on screen.
- **A short cut is a narrative, not a montage**: build, multiply, improve, brand. Drop ideas, keep the arc.

## 3. The script

One row per beat, written before the beat map, which is built from it:

| Beat | Time | Picture | Words (voice line or caption) | Motif |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

- Write the words, then time them. Spoken: about 2.5 words a second. Read on screen: about 0.3 s a
  word, at least 1.5 s a line. The words set the length; if they do not fit the recommended length,
  cut words, not the reading time. When the timed words land below the format's range, the words
  win: keep the shorter length, never pad, and say so in the brief (the recommended length sized the
  scope, not a target).
- Read every line aloud. Anything you stumble on, rewrite.
- Cut a third on the second pass. Every word has to earn its screen time. Cut filler, never an
  instruction or an exact UI label; in a tutorial the stranger test decides what stays.
- A voiced line and its picture land together (within 0.3 s); a caption lands with a still picture
  (promo) or with its action (tutorial), per the module's profile.

## 4. The copy

**The hook** (the first 3 s). Write five, pick one, and show the user the best two or three:
- specific and concrete: a number, a name, a real situation ("Re: Re: Re: Intro call", not "Scheduling
  is hard");
- about the viewer ("you", their problem), not the company;
- one idea, 7 words or fewer on screen, landed within a second;
- tension or curiosity, never a riddle: the viewer knows what it is about.

**Captions and lines.**
- The product's own words and claims (its site, its UI labels). Tutorials use the exact labels.
- Verbs over nouns; plain words over jargon; one accent word per caption, the one that matters.
- Banned filler: "streamline", "revolutionize", "seamless", "unlock", "leverage", "game-changer",
  "all-in-one", "next-level", "effortless", "powerful". If a rival could say the line, rewrite it.
- No invented numbers, testimonials or logos presented as real; fictional names beside illustrative
  numbers.
- The brand's voice: take its tone from its site and past posts; a playful brand is not made formal,
  a serious one not made cute.

**The call to action**: one verb and one destination ("Book your first call at <your domain>"). Not two.

**Other languages**: localize, do not translate word for word; re-time every line (Hebrew and German
run longer), and re-check the hook still lands in a second.

## 5. Approval and critique

- The script table, the chosen hook (and its runners-up) and the CTA go into the brief for approval,
  before stills.
- The critique loop scores **story and copy** for every format: is the one message clear after one
  viewing, does the hook make a stranger stay, is every line specific to this brand, does the CTA name
  one action. Tests: read it aloud; the stranger test (someone new says what it is, who it is for, what
  to do); the rival test (would a competitor's logo fit these words?).
