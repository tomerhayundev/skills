# Spam guard

When an ask, or one of your own ideas, matches an item below, do not do it and do not recommend it. Say so in plain words, give the reason with its date, and offer the honest alternative. One short paragraph, no lecture. Each reason rests on a dated line in facts.md.

<!-- myths -->
1. **Buying or selling links, or "authority placements" that pass ranking credit.**
   Why: this is link spam; paid links must carry rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow" (Google spam policies, 2026-08-28; facts.md).
   Instead: linkable assets other sites want to cite, digital PR, real partnerships.
2. **PBNs, link exchanges and tiered links.**
   Why: link spam. Google's spam policies name link exchanges; they do not name PBNs or tiered links, which fall under the general clause (an inference from the policy text) on links made mainly to manipulate rankings (2026-08-28; facts.md). The links exist to pass ranking credit, not because readers found something worth linking.
   Instead: the same alternatives as item 1.
3. **Parasite pages on other sites' domains** to borrow their authority.
   Why: this is the site reputation policy (the heading was "site reputation abuse" before; "parasite SEO" is industry slang, not Google's term), and it can draw a manual action. Inside the EEA enforcement works differently from 2026-08-30; outside the EEA, Israel included, the earlier enforcement applies (Google, 2026-08-28; facts.md).
   Instead: publish on the site's own domain; earn coverage elsewhere with real expertise.
4. **Mass pages, fan-out page farms and town-swapped location pages.**
   Why: scaled content abuse is producing many pages mainly to manipulate rankings with little added value, by any method, AI included (2026-08-28). A separate page for every query fan-out variation, mainly to manipulate rankings or AI answers, is the same (2026-07-10).
   Instead: fewer pages with unique value: one per real offer or real place, with its own facts, photos, prices and local detail.
5. **Manufactured mentions, forum and Reddit astroturfing, self-promotional "best of" lists placed for AI.**
   Why: Google's spam policies cover attempts to manipulate generative AI responses since 2026-05-15, and Google lists seeking inauthentic mentions as not needed. Mentions correlate with AI visibility, but that is a correlation, not a cause (study, 2025-12-12).
   Instead: real community participation under the real name, earned media, reviews from real customers.
6. **Fake, gated, selectively requested or incentivized reviews, and review markup a business writes about itself.**
   Why: Google's review snippet guidelines say not to include fake reviews or undisclosed incentivized reviews, on the page or in structured data (2026-07-24; facts.md); contribution policies prohibit incentivized or biased reviews and rating manipulation, and a 2026 update bars asking only happy customers (trade-press report, 2026-02-20). A review a business writes about itself and shows as a customer's is a fake review (inferred from that guideline).
   Instead: ask every customer the same way, with no reward, and show what comes back.
7. **Back-button hijacking, including from ad scripts.**
   Why: a malicious-practices violation enforced from 2026-06-15; third-party and ad scripts can cause it.
   Instead: test pages with every ad and tag script on, and remove any script that changes where Back goes.
8. **Cloaking and sneaky redirects.**
   Why: both are listed in Google's spam policies (2026-08-28; facts.md): showing crawlers and people different content or destinations is a violation.
   Instead: one page for everyone; hreflang for languages and regions.
9. **Keyword stuffing and keyword density targets.**
   Why: keyword stuffing is a spam-policy violation (2026-08-28; facts.md), and Google has no notion of an optimal keyword density, so a density target is a myth (2023-01-31).
   Instead: answer the buyer's question in the words customers use.
10. **"LSI keywords".**
    Why: there is no such thing (Google, 2023-01-31).
    Instead: cover the subtopics and questions buyers actually ask.
11. **FAQ or HowTo markup to win rich results.**
    Why: FAQ rich results ended for all sites on 2026-05-07 (limited since 2023-08-08); HowTo rich results were removed in September 2023. FAQPage stays valid schema.org markup.
    Instead: write the Q&A for readers if it helps them; any markup must match the visible content.
12. **llms.txt as a ranking or citation lever.**
    Why: Google Search ignores it; the crawler docs of OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity do not describe reading it; a study of about 300,000 domains found no effect on AI citations (2025-11-07).
    Instead: check crawler access, main content in the HTML, and citable facts. Harmless and optional if the owner wants the file for agents.
13. **Special AI markup, or chunking content for AI, as a requirement.**
    Why: Google lists special schema or markup, chunking content and writing specially for AI as not needed (2026-07-10).
    Instead: clear writing, answer first (writing.md).
14. **Blocking Google-Extended to leave AI Overviews.**
    Why: it controls Gemini training and grounding only; it does not affect Search, AI Overviews or AI Mode (2026-07-14).
    Instead: the Search generative AI setting in Search Console (since 2026-08-31; not a ranking signal), or nosnippet, data-nosnippet and max-snippet.
15. **Invented experts, credentials, reviewers, statistics or studies.**
    Why: every claim must be fact-checked, and E-E-A-T, which raters use, puts trust first (Google, 2026-10-01 and 2025-12-10). The GEO paper's "up to 40%" gains came from prompts that asked the model to add invented quotes and statistics.
    Instead: a real author with real experience, the owner's own data, sourced figures, `[ADD: ...]` placeholders.
16. **Promising rankings, AI citations or traffic numbers.**
    Why: Google says no third-party tool has access to its internal ranking data or AI systems (2026-06-05); a 2026 survey of 45 GEO studies found no technique with a stable causal effect across platforms (2026-07-15); AI answers vary run to run.
    Instead: promise the process: what will change, how it will be judged, and when.
