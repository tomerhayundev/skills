# How people decide (used honestly)

People do not decide by reading every option. These principles make the next action easy and worth taking.
Every one has an honest form and a manipulative one; the skill uses only the honest form. No fake urgency, fake
scarcity, fake progress, fake results or fake reviews (meter rules UNSOURCED_NUMBER and UNSOURCED_QUOTE), and no
shaming dismiss buttons ("No thanks, I like paying more", "I'll risk it").

| Principle | Do | On a phone | On a desktop | Never |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Smart defaults** | Pre-select the most common choice in every field (tomorrow, 2 people, pickup at the usual time) so the visitor scans and adjusts instead of filling from nothing. | Choices as tappable chips and steppers, not dropdowns or typing; the next few time slots as buttons with "all times" after them. | The same chips in one row; a calendar may sit open beside the form. | A default the business does not actually want most people to take, or a pre-ticked paid extra. |
| **A button that says the result** | Say what happens and, when it is true, how many: "See 12 free tables", "Order 3 loaves". | Full width at the bottom, inside thumb reach; sticky when the form is long. | Next to what it acts on, never alone at the far end of a wide row. | A count you do not have. |
| **Choose by seeing** | When the choice is visual (a table, a seat, a cake size, a colour), show it as a picture or a plan, not a list of names. | One visual choice per screen. | The visual and the summary side by side. | A picture that hides a choice a keyboard user can make. |
| **Never start at zero** | In a multi-step form, show named steps and count what the visitor already did (they chose the product: step one is done). | A short step bar at the top: names, not only dots. | Steps beside the form. | Progress that is not real, or steps that are padded. |
| **Value before the ask** | Show prices, the menu, the result or a real sample before asking for contact details; ask afterwards, saying what they get. | The useful part first, the ask under it. | The result and the ask side by side. | Results blurred behind a sign-up, or a newsletter popup on the first visit. |
| **Let them build it** | Let the visitor shape their order, box or booking before any details are asked; show it as theirs, with a live preview that updates with each choice. | Each choice on its own row with the preview pinned on top. | The preview beside the choices. | Asking for an account before anything is built. |
| **Real losses only** | When something can really be missed (Friday orders close Thursday 12:00, the last seats of an event), say it plainly with the real date; when the visitor's own work is at stake, name it ("your 3 saved loaves"). | One line near the button. | One line near the button. | Countdowns that reset, invented "only 2 left", fear copy, shaming the "no" option, spin-the-wheel discounts, "forever" discounts, "claim your gift before it's gone". |
| **Price in context** | Show a price next to what it relates to: the add-on under the main item, the price per person or per loaf, the yearly total beside the monthly one; the total on the button ("Pay 48 ₪"). Show two plans (the usual one marked), the rest behind "All plans"; a yearly plan also shown per month; "No commitment, cancel any time" under the button when it is true. | The context line right under the price. | A comparison table when there are plans. | A decoy plan nobody should buy, or hiding the total. |
| **The outcome first** | Open with what changes for the visitor, in their terms ("Fresh bread at your door by 8"), then how; features come after. When the visitor answered questions, build the promise from their answers. | One line under the heading. | The outcome beside a picture of it. | A generic "the perfect plan for you" or an outcome you cannot deliver. |
| **Say how long and what happens when** | Before a form or a trial, say the steps and the time ("3 steps, about 2 minutes"), the first marked "now". For a trial, deposit or subscription, a dated timeline beside the button (today, the reminder, the charge) and one sentence on how to cancel. | The timeline above the button. | The timeline beside the form. | A reminder you will not send, or a cancel path harder than the sign-up. |
| **One question per screen** | In a quiz, booking or sign-up longer than three fields, one question per step with answers as pictures or short rows; end with a result made from the answers. | One question fills the screen, the answer buttons in thumb reach. | One step per view, the summary growing at the side. | More steps than the answers need, or questions whose answers are never used. |
| **Ask at the right moment** | Ask for an account, a newsletter or a permission only after the value, and show what it brings (a sample of the message they would get); the decline is a plain "Not now". | Under the result, never over it. | Beside the result. | Asking on arrival, or a decline that shames. |

## How to apply

- At brief time, list the decisions the visitor makes on the way to the one action (which loaf, which day, how
  many) and design each with the table above.
- Put each decision where the visitor's thumb or eye already is: phone first, then widen.
- Check on the walk: on a phone, can the one action be done with one thumb, without typing what a choice could
  give? On a desktop, does the visitor see the choice and its result at once?
